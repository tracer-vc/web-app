**Core Evaluation Principles** 

- Evaluation \-\> guided by set of core principles  
- Each principle \-\> specifies a lens through which a company is assed & operationalized later (via specific evaluation dimensions/questions)  
- This represents the backbone of the funds investment thesis 

| Principle  | Description  |
| :---- | :---- |
| Problem Criticality  | Distinguish between solutions to high urgency, high-cost problems and optional improvements. Prioritize the depth of the problem over its immediate breadth: a smaller market with an acute need and strong willingness to pay can be more attractive than a large market where adoption is optional  |
| Founder Market Fit and Leadership Continuity  | Evaluate the team's suitability for the specific problem domain. Assess whether the founding team has credible technical or market specific experience and whether leadership is aligned with the long horizon demands of building deep capabilities  |

**Standardized Evaluation Outputs**

- Evaluation \-\> produces three standardized outputs   
  - (1) one-page Thesis Card   
  - (2) Evidence Pack \-\> backs the Thesis Card (claim-evidence traceability)  
  - (3) Decision Snapshot \-\> converts analysis into actionable recommendation   
- Identifier Conventions  
  - Sources: S1, S2, …, Sn (Source Table)  
  - Claims: C1, C2, …, Cn (Claim Table)  
  - Conflicts: CR1, CR2, .., CRn (Conflicts Register/Table)  
  - Uncertainties/Open Questions: U1, U2, …, U3 (Uncertainty List/Table)  

**Standardized Evaluation Outputs: Thesis Card**

- Thesis Card \-\> summarizes the evaluation (standardized one-page document)  
- Presents evidence-linked testable thesis statement with specific scenarios, falsifiers and evidence backed dimension scores   
  - Thesis statements \= concise testable explanation of why a company should succeed, specifying (i) proposed value creation mechanism (2) conditions required for success (ii) observable implications that could confirm or falsify the thesis over time   
  - falsifiers \= specific, observable condition defined in advance \-\> if met \-\> proves thesis wrong or necessitates a decision to pass  
- Formatting:   
  - Length \-\> max. 1 Page  
  - Conciseness \-\> entries restricted to 1-2 Sentences   
  - Traceability \-\> all evaluative statements must cite claims IDs from the Claim Table   
- Evidence Linking   
  - Evidence \= external data, documents, observable facts used to validate a claim; is distinct from the claim itself; must be traceable to a specific verifiable origin rather than a general impression   
  - Content fields: 

| Field | Content requirement  |
| :---- | :---- |
| One-sentence thesis | Concise, testable explanation \-\> why company can achieve outlier *(= unusually large success case, located in the extreme upper tail of a heavy-tailed distribution; can contribute a disproportionate share of total returns)* outcome; specifying core mechanism & necessary conditions for that  |
| Outlier scenario  | State outlier outcome magnitude (5-10 year time horizon) & mechanism that would produce it |
| Base / Upside / Failure case | Three concise outlines, one for each scenario & each must name 1-3 gating variables *(= external condition or dependency that must resolve favorably)* & cite supporting claims from the claims table; Failure must align with strongest counter case (= strongest possible set of reasons why a thesis might be wrong) |
| Moat hypothesis | Specify claimed moat mechanism & why it strengthens over time?  |
| Entry wedge | Specify initial buyers and their use case as well as the sales motion channel and corresponding adoption trigger  |
| Falsifiers  | 2-4 observable conditions that would reject/materially revise the thesis  |
| De-risking milestones  | 2-3 milestones that would materially reduce uncertainty  |
| Dimension Scores (in depth analysis) | Score each Dimension based on its questions (0-5). For each Dimension: cite 2-5 claim IDs & name strongest counter signal  |
| Open Questions | List decision-critical uncertainties & min. Evidence checks to resolve them  |

**Standardized Evaluation Outputs: Evidence Pack** 

- Evidence Pack \= complete verification layer for evaluation   
- Makes the analysis auditable by:  
  - enforcing claim evidence-linking  
  - Explicit conflict handling of sources & claims   
  - Confidence Calibration (= process of assigning a reliability label \[High, Medium, Low\] to a claim \-\> based on  quality & independence of it’s supporting evidence)  
- **Structure**   
  - Evidence pack is constructed sequentially   
  - (1) creating the source table  
  - (2) generating the claims table based on the source list  
  - (3) recording arising conflicts in a conflict register  
- **Components**   
  - Source table (S\#)  
    -  \= standardized inventory \-\> all external materials used during the evaluation \-\> establishes provenance of every subsequent claims   
  - Claim table (C\#)   
    - Claim \= atomic testable proposition extracted from sources or inferred from data; fundamental units of the thesis; each claim must be distinct traceable to specific verifiable origin & assigned a confidence level  
    - core verification output  
    - containing atomic propositions (claims) extracted from the source table  
  - Conflict Register (CR\#)  
    - \= Log for tracking material disagreements between sources or claims   
    - Prevents suppression of contradictory evidence  
    - Conflicts \-\> require explicit resolution status rather than narrative smoothing  
  - Uncertainty List (U\#)  
    - \= catalog of unresolved questions & risks that could impact investment thesis   
    - Each item (question/risk/uncertainty) is prioritized & linked to potential falsification check  
  - Dimension Assessment   
    - \= detailed analytical record containing specific answers to all diagnostic questions of all evaluation dimensions  
    - Evaluation Dimension \=    
    - Preserves the long form reasoning & evidence synthesis \-\> that supports the summary score on the Thesis Card 

**Standardized Evaluation Outputs: Decision Snapshot**

- Synthesises Thesis Card into definitive screening outcome   
- Standalone summary \-\> presenting classification, core arguments & critical uncertainties with direct citations to underlying Evidence Pack  
- **Classification** \-\> select one of the output states below \-\> based on holistic synthesis of all Evaluation Dimensions \-\> *user can set under which circumstances the system applies which state.* But find a couple of examples of how that could look like below:   
  - **Proceed**: thesis demonstrates consistent case across all dimensions \-\> supporting credible outlier (projected return justifies acceptance of remaining uncertainty); no dimension rated as disqualifying   
  - **Watch**: thesis clearly demonstrates potential across the dimensions but currently constrained by specific Gating Variable   
  - **Pass:** thesis contains structured flaw (any given dimension has a disqualifying rating)  
- Classification must be justified \-\> 1-2 sentences citing relevant dimensions scores   
- **Components**  
  - Core Investment Thesis:   
    - three strongest evidence based arguments supporting the outlier scenario (each in single sentence & linked to specific claim ID)  
  - Primary Risks & Counter Case   
    - Most critical structural weaknesses & falsification criteria \-\> identified during evaluation (cited via claim IDs or uncertainty IDs)  
  - Forward Research Agenda   
    - 3-7 specific open questions \+ specific evidence required to resolve them   
  - Re-evaluation Trigger   
    - Specific Gating Variable & observable event/timeframe that would necessitate re-assessment of the opportunity

**Framework Application Workflow: (3) Claim Extraction and Structuring** 

- Convert information from source table into **Atomic Testable Claims**   
- Rules for **Atomic Claims:** 	  
  - (1) One statement per claim \-\> each claim expresses exactly one proposition    
  - (2) Concrete & falsifiable-\> specific entities, quantities, dates & observable outcomes over vague adjectives   
  - (3) Level of Inference (claim types) \-\>  label:  
    - (i) Fact \-\> directly supported by a source  
    - (ii) Inference \-\> derived from multiple facts   
    - (iii) Speculation \-\> explicit hypothesis   
  - (4) Evidence traceability   
    - (i) Facts \-\> must  be linked to at least one entry in the source table / source id (S\#)  
    - (ii) Inferences \-\> must reference the supporting source table entries / source ids (S\#)  
    - (iii) speculation \-\> must be explicitly marked as such   
  - (5) Conflict Awareness \-\> if claims disagree \-\> record both claims in conflict register   
- **Extraction procedure** (How to extract claims from sources)  
  - (1) For each **Source** in **Source Table** \-\> system extracts 2-6 most decision-relevant statements as candidate claims   
  - (2) System rewrites candidates into atomic claims (one proposition each) & assigns claim type label (Fact/Inference/Speculation)  
  - (3) For Facts & Inference type claims system attaches substantiating source ids (S\#)  
  - (4) System assigns a confidence level (High \[H\] / Medium \[M\] / Low \[L\]). In the table below you can see what each of those confidence levels actually mean:

| Confidence Level  | Meaning  |
| :---- | :---- |
| High \[H\] | Supported by Tier 1 sources and/or multiple independent sources  |
| Medium \[M\]  | Supported by Tier 1 sources but with limitations (indirect, somewhat vague, older, partially conflicting) or supported by Tier 2 sources  |
| Low \[L\]) | Supported only by Tier 3 sources  |

  - (5) System merges duplicate claims

- **Dimension Scoring**   
  - Dimension Scores \-\> not meant to produce certainty \-\> meant to make reasoning **comparable**, **evidence-linked** & **falsifiable**   
  - For the system to apply a specific score to a dimension \-\> each score has an **Anchor Assumption** that is defined by the user in settings. Find an example of those anchor assumptions in the table below 


| Score | Anchor interpretation |
| :---- | :---- |
| 0-1 | The dimension is not meaningfully supported: the case is missing, contradicted by evidence, or dominated by the counter-case. |
| 2 | The dimension has some positive signals, but support remains shallow, weakly evidenced, or highly fragile. |
| 3 | The dimension is credibly supported: a coherent case exists and is linked to some evidence, but important uncertainties remain. |
| 4 | The dimension is strongly supported: key claims are evidenced, the mechanism is clear, and the case holds against credible counter-arguments. |
| 5 | The dimension is exceptionally supported: evidence is strong, the underlying mechanism is compelling, and the dimension materially strengthens the outlier thesis.  |

    - (4) identifies the strongest counter-signal for that dimension.
